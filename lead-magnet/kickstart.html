Step 1

Your calorie & protein starting point

Fat loss happens when you consistently take in a little less energy than you use. “A little” is the key word — big cuts are hard to keep up.

1. Estimate your maintenance calories

A quick, rough estimate for many adults:

Using kilograms
kg × 31–35

Lower end if you sit most of the day, higher end if you’re on your feet a lot.

Using pounds
lb × 14–16

Same idea — it’s a starting estimate, not an exact number.

2. Create a modest deficit

Subtract roughly 300–500 calories per day from your maintenance estimate. Very low intakes aren’t recommended without professional supervision.

3. Set your protein

Protein helps you stay full and protects muscle while you lose fat. A common target is 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight per day (about 0.7–1 g per lb). If you have a lot of weight to lose, base it on your goal weight instead.

Worked example

Body weight 80 kg, mostly sitting → maintenance ≈ 80 × 32 = 2,560 kcal.
Modest deficit → about 2,100–2,250 kcal per day.
Protein → 80 × 1.6–2.0 = about 130–160 g per day (roughly 35–40 g per meal across 4 meals).

These are estimates — your real results over 1–2 weeks matter more (see page 8).

Step 2

Build a plate that keeps you full

You don’t need to weigh everything in week one. Use this plate at most meals:

  • ½ plate vegetables or salad — volume and fibre for very few calories.
  • ¼ plate protein — about a palm or two.
  • ¼ plate smart carbs — rice, potatoes, oats, bread, fruit.
  • A thumb of fats — olive oil, nuts, avocado.
  • Drink water with the meal.

Step 4

The daily habits that multiply results

Sleep

Most adults need 7–9 hours. Poor sleep makes hunger and cravings harder to manage.

Steps

Check your current daily average, then add 1,000–2,000 steps. Small and steady wins.

Water

Keep a bottle nearby and drink with every meal. Needs vary with heat and activity.

Make habits automatic

  • Stack it: “After I pour my morning coffee, I fill my water bottle.”
  • Walk after meals: 10 minutes after lunch or dinner adds up fast.
  • Wind down: screens off 30 minutes before bed, same bedtime most nights.
  • Prep the easy option: washed fruit and a protein snack you can grab.
  • Never miss twice: a missed day is normal — just get back to it tomorrow.

Why this matters: calories and training set the direction, but sleep, movement and hydration decide how easy it feels to stay consistent. Consistency is what drives results.

ZZmaFitTrack & adjust

Check in. Adjust calmly.

Body weight goes up and down every day because of water, salt and food volume. Look at trends, not single days.

What to track

  • Weekly average weight: weigh 3+ mornings a week (after the bathroom, before eating) and compare weekly averages.
  • Waist: measure once a week at belly-button height.
  • Your checklist score: how many boxes did you tick?
  • Energy, sleep and hunger: a quick 1–5 rating is enough.

If progress slows after 2 weeks

First, be honest about consistency — if the checklist score was low, focus on that before changing anything. If you were consistent and the weekly averages haven’t moved, change one thing:

Option A

Reduce daily intake by about 100–200 calories.

Option B

Add about 2,000 steps per day.

Then give it another 1–2 weeks before adjusting again. Small, patient changes beat big swings.
